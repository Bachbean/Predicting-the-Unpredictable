# The Three Body Problem

Beginning with Newton's laws of motion, one of the most obvious questions in planetary motion would be to describe and predict a system of two bodies of similar mass, called the two-body problem. Newton and even others before him were able to completely describe these systems in closed form equations that had a finite number of terms, hence the two-body problem was considered solved. But Newton and the scientists in the 300 years since have been unable to find such a general solution to the n-body problem for n greater than 2. 

Save for certain specific configurations that were discovered by computers, and that have essentially no chance of appearing in nature, all configurations of three masses of similar size were shown to collapse and be statistically random over long timescales. However, perturbation theory has allowed us to model the restricted 3-body problem to a high degree of accuracy, when one mass is much smaller than the other two, and modern computers combined with numerical integration made short term models quite precise.

One byproduct of 3-body systems that don't end with the bodies colliding is the production of hypervelocity bodies or stars, where one mass gets ejected at extremely high speeds, and an interesting science fiction idea was to try to harness these bodies to travel between galaxies.
